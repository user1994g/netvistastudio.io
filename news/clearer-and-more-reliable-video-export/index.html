    Two focused betas make video export clearer and more reliable

    Resolution selection moved earlier, export progress became easier to follow, and longer Windows/Linux renders received a reliability fix.

    By NetVista Studio · Published 21 October 2026

    Recorded milestone
    20–28 August 2026
    Version / context
    1.4 Betas 1–2
    A retrospective of an earlier development milestone.

    This article was first published on 21 October 2026. The earlier milestone date describes the development record, not an earlier publication of this article.

    Choose the output before its destination

    The first NetVista Studio 1.4 beta, published on 20 August 2026, concentrated on the export flow across macOS, Windows and Linux. It asked for the finished movie's resolution before asking where to save it, making that choice explicit rather than leaving users to discover it later in delivery.

    The chooser offered 720p, 1080p, 1440p, 4K, 8K, 16K and custom dimensions. The selected size then continued into the existing format, codec, frame-rate, audio, progress and cancellation workflow. Windows and Linux packages were also arranged to extract into a clean application folder.

    Make progress and failures visible

    Beta 2 followed on 28 August. Starting an export switched to the Delivery page so its progress was visible. A completed movie produced a confirmation and could be revealed in Finder on Mac. Failed exports surfaced the underlying error instead of appearing to stop without explanation.

    For Windows and Linux, the release fixed a potential FFmpeg pipe stall during longer renders. This was a targeted reliability change, not a claim that every possible format, project or very large export had been certified. Both betas retained their early-access status and the request to back up project and media files.

    A useful way to test delivery

    The practical lesson is to check a short sequence before committing to a long render. Choose an output that suits the source and intended destination, then inspect the exported movie's picture, sound and timing. Increasing the resolution does not create detail absent from the original footage, and the largest presets still depend on encoder capabilities and available resources. Keep the editable project as well as the exported movie.
