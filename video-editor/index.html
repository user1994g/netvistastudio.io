    THE DESKTOP EDITING WORKSPACE

    From clips to a finished video.

    Multitrack video editing

    Arrange several clips side by side or on separate video and audio tracks. Trim, split, move and snap clips into place. Zoom the timeline when you need precise cuts, then zoom out to see the full sequence.

    Colour grading and LUTs

    Adjust the look of your footage with colour controls and imported .cube LUTs. The Mac colour workspace includes lift, gamma and gain wheels, curves, scopes and grading nodes for more detailed adjustments.

    Effects and keyframes

    Animate position, scale, rotation and opacity to change a shot over time. Work with effects such as blur, sharpen and vignette, and use green-screen keying when combining supported footage.

    Sound alongside the picture

    Place audio clips on the timeline, adjust their timing and work with linked video and sound. Keep music and other audio on separate tracks so you can build the sequence around the picture.

    Editable projects

    On desktop, save your work in the native .netvistastudio project format. A project keeps the edit and settings so you can continue later; an exported movie is the version you share or publish. Keep your source media available when reopening a project.

    Video export options

    Choose a resolution and frame rate for your final movie. Desktop export presets include 1080p, 4K, 8K and 16K. Very large exports depend on your computer, available memory and codec support; 16K is not guaranteed on every system.

    CHOOSE YOUR DEVICE

    Desktop creative tools. Core mobile editing.

    Download the native package for your operating system after signing in. Beta features differ across platforms: the Mac app includes the more developed colour, photo and animated 3D workspaces, while the Windows and Linux beta uses FFmpeg for its media workflow. The first standalone iPadOS and Android betas focus on local video import, trim, reorder, save and export; they do not include the complete desktop toolset or require a running Mac.

    macOS

    The current Mac download is for Apple Silicon. It uses native Mac editing and media interfaces, with additional colour, photo and 3D scene tools.

    Windows

    Choose the Windows beta package for the desktop editor. Follow its included setup instructions and verify media and export support with a short test project.

    Linux

    Choose the Linux beta package. Follow the bundled instructions for the runtime and FFmpeg dependencies on your distribution.

    iPadOS 16+

    A first native iPad editor with local video import, full-sequence preview, trim, reorder, project save and MP4 export at 720p, 1080p or 4K. Install the IPA through AltStore Classic for re-signing. Device codec and storage limits apply.

    Android 8.0+ / Samsung

    A first native Android editor with local video import, sequence preview, trim, reorder, project save and MP4 export. Install the signed APK with the normal Android package installer. Supported encoders vary by device.

    Mobile project formats are not interchangeable with desktop projects or with the other mobile edition. Advanced grading/effects, multitrack compositing, photo, 3D, Game Maker and mods are not included in this first mobile beta. Keep project backups and test a short export on your device.

    BEFORE YOUR FIRST EDIT

    Questions about NetVista.

    Is NetVista Studio free?

    The current public beta is free to download. Sign in or create a NetVista account on the download page to choose a system package.

    Is this a browser video editor?

    The website explains the apps and handles account access. Editing runs in the installed native desktop or mobile app, with your project files on your own device. The standalone mobile editors are not the Mac browser companion.

    Can I edit more than one video clip?

    Yes. The desktop timeline supports multiple clips and tracks for layering picture and sound. The first mobile beta supports a sequence of clips with trim and reorder, not multitrack compositing.

    How do I start editing a video?

    Install the package for your system, open Video Editor, import a short clip and arrange it on the timeline. Our getting-started guide walks through a first project, saving and exporting.

    Is the beta ready for important work?

    It is still developing. Keep copies of your media and saved projects, and test a small export before relying on the beta for a larger edit.
